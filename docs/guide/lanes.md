# Lanes

Lanes run Aether's own calls on another coding plan you pay for, while that plan's quota lasts. The `lanes` and `harnesses` options that turn them on are in [Options](options.md#options); this page is how they behave.

## Running Aether's calls on other plans

### Lanes

A lane runs Aether's own calls on another coding plan the owner pays for, while that plan's quota lasts. agy, the Antigravity CLI, was the first; Codex, Grok, Cursor and Muse have adapters too. See the [lanes spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-08-lanes-design.md) and phase 2b of the [RADAR roadmap](../roadmap/2026-10-10-radar.md).

- **Which harnesses.** The plugin's `harnesses` option lists the harnesses lanes may use (`harnesses`, `agy` by default; the names are `agy, codex, grok, cursor, muse`). Every one whose CLI is installed and whose table entry has a model for the call's native model and role is a candidate for that call. `aether-plan compile` records the candidates of each role and native model.
- **The order.** The quota decides among the candidates of one call, never the level: they serve the same role and replace the same native model, and a test checks the table against the models index (`plugins/aether/config/models/models.json`: current, offered by the harness, with a profile). The candidates go in this order: first the groups with a reading, the highest priority first; in a tie, the nearer reset, then the order of `harnesses`; then the groups with no reading, in the order of `harnesses`. A group under the reserve, or with a dry mark, is left out. The priority of a group is the share left of its longest window (the weekly one) divided by the hours to its reset, at least one hour: the quota most at risk of being lost goes first, and the shares of different plans compare equal. The workflow walks the order for each call: the first candidate whose pool has room takes it; if its start is unavailable, for any reason, the next is tried; the call runs native, at its own level, only when none is left.
- **Who takes a call.** `plugins/aether/config/lanes.json` names the external models of each harness. For agy: Gemini 3.8 Flash for Haiku implementers, and agy's own Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 for the rest; Gemini 4 Argon takes design seats only. agy's `high` runs Claude's `high` and `xhigh`; calls at `max`, and `ops`, are always native. For Grok: `grok-4.7` for Haiku implementers (checked against grok 1.0.41 on 2026-10-13; `grok-4.7-build-fast` is the same model faster and at twice the price, so it is not a lane). Codex and Cursor have entries without models: their model ids depend on your account and the CLI's version, so you add them once you have checked them (below). Muse's entry has one, `muse-wonder-full-1.0`, the only model its CLI lists to the owner's account, which replaces Opus by the owner's decision (see [Muse](#muse)).
- **Quota.** A group is used while every window of it, such as agy's 5 hours and weekly, stays above a 5% reserve (`reserve` in the table) and no dry mark holds it. A call that ends for quota, or a start that finds the group dry, leaves a dry mark in the plugin's data directory, so it outlives the wave and the run: until the group's reset when a reading with a balance shows the group under the reserve, and otherwise for 60 minutes (`dryMarkMinutes`), renewed if the next call in the group ends for quota again; a fresh reading that shows the group healthy lifts a mark that came from a reading (below). A reading that launches a process, agy's, is kept for its life (`quotaLifeMinutes`: 10 minutes) and served to the starts and to `status`, so they launch none inside it; a finished agy call and `aether-lane quota` take a fresh reading, which renews the kept one. Grok's reading is not kept: its 30 minutes (`quotaLifeMinutes`) are the age after which its log line is stale and a wave refreshes it. Grok's weekly share stays valid until its week ends. Codex and Cursor report no quota, nor does Grok without a reading: each is tried after the groups that have one, until a call fails for quota. Muse's is the one its last call reported, and unknown before that call (see [Muse](#muse)).
- **agy's quotas and its refusals.** agy has individual quotas that its `/usage` does not show: it lists two groups, `Gemini Models` (Flash and Argon together, since agy 1.4.2) and `Claude and GPT models`, so the table has two keys for agy, `gemini` and `claude`. `groups` maps each key to the `/usage` group it reads, and several keys may read one group (each gets the same windows, with its own mark, place in the order and choice), so a later split of the Gemini key is one line of `lanes.json`. What tells that a quota is gone is agy's refusal, `Individual quota reached. ... Resets in 163h22m27s.`: a lane call or a design seat that agy refuses leaves a dry mark on the group of the model it called, whatever `/usage` shows. The mark holds 60 minutes (`dryMarkMinutes`), not the reset the message states: that reset was wrong once (agy refused Argon with "Resets in 163h" and answered three days later) and agy's quotas change between versions, while a refused call costs a failed process and no tokens, so the group is tried again every hour. `aether-lane quota` shows that the mark comes from a refusal, with the reset agy stated as stated. A design seat skips a group a dry mark still holds and goes to the next model of its list, or to the native agent. **Marks heal.** A mark that came from a reading is lifted when a fresh reading, taken after the mark and younger than `quotaLifeMinutes`, shows every window above the reserve: a lane call's start, `aether-lane quota` and `status`, the router and a design seat's choice all check it, because providers renew a quota without saying so. A refusal's mark is never lifted by a reading, since `/usage` does not show an individual limit. An unconfirmed mark never holds longer than the minutes it was given, whatever its file says. A mark file of a group the table does not have is ignored.
- **Fallback.** When a harness is missing, out of quota or fails to start, the same call goes to the next candidate, and to Aether's own agent when none is left, without spending the ladder. A lane that fails after it started goes to the native agent. `aether-plan compile` names each enabled harness that takes no call, and why.
- **Containment.** A lane works without permission prompts in the unit's worktree: agy with `--dangerously-skip-permissions`, Codex in its `workspace-write` sandbox, Grok with `--always-approve`, Cursor with `--force`, Muse with `--disable-approval` and its sandbox on. Git checks catch changes outside the task's Owns, to the integration worktree and branch, and from read-only roles. The lane's environment drops variables named like tokens, keys and secrets, points `gh`, `npm`, `docker`, `gcloud`, `aws` and `kubectl` away from your credentials, and sets git's `protocol.allow` to `never`, so a plain git command in the lane cannot reach a remote. Your home directory stays, because each CLI keeps its own login there. So this prevents accidents, not an agent that overrides these settings or goes looking for your files. Nothing undoes a network action, so you turn on each harness yourself.
- **Report.** `aether-report` shows, per group, its calls, the quota consumed, the share left of each window at the start and at the end of the run and the next reset; the dollars at list price, apart from native Claude; each harness's calls; and what every lane call chose (`lanes.run.choices`: the candidates in order with their priority, the lane that took the call or none, and why). The same choices are in the lane log, one entry per call. `aether-lane status <run>` gives, per group, what `aether-lane quota` does plus its dry mark, and, per role and native model, the order of the candidates.
- **Off for one run:** `aether-plan compile <plan.md> --no-lanes`.
- **Off by default.** Lanes run only when the plugin's `lanes` option is on (`lanes`, off by default; see [Options](options.md#options)).
- **Linux only.** Lanes need Linux, because stopping a lane's processes reads `/proc`.

**Reading the quota.** `aether-lane quota [<harness>...] [--refresh] [--json]` needs no run. For each harness, by default the installed ones, it prints each group's windows: the share left, the reset in UTC and local time, the hours left, the priority, whether the group is usable above the reserve and with no dry mark, and where the reading came from and how old it is. agy's reading is its `/usage`. **Grok's quota is read over ACP**: grok answers the request `_x.ai/billing` on `grok agent stdio` with the config its UI logs as `billing: fetched credits config` (about a second, no UI, no session and no model call), and the request also leaves that line in `~/.grok/logs/unified.jsonl`, where `status` and `start` read it; they call it stale after 30 minutes. `aether-lane quota --refresh`, and `aether-lane status <run> --refresh`, the quota step of each wave in the workflow, ask grok over ACP (10 seconds at most); if that gives no answer, an error or no config, the fallback opens grok's UI for a few seconds (20 at most) in an empty temporary directory, with no prompt, and removes the session folder grok made for that directory, and no other. A `status` run by hand does not refresh. A line without `creditUsagePercent` is read as 0% used, and says so wherever it is shown (spec 002, A3). Aether never refreshes inside a lane call.

**Adding a harness.** A harness is an adapter, `plugins/aether/lib/harness/<name>.js`, and an entry in the table, and its name goes in `HARNESS_NAMES` and `FACTORY` of `plugins/aether/lib/harness/index.js`, or the `harnesses` option drops it; the order rule does not change. The adapter's `quota()` gives, per group, its windows (`remaining` from 0 to 1 and `resetsAt`) or `unknown` when it cannot read them, which puts the harness after the groups with a reading. An adapter whose reading launches a process sets `quotaCost: 'process'`, and Aether keeps the reading for `quotaLifeMinutes`.

**Turning on Codex, Grok, Cursor or Muse.** Their adapters follow Spec Kit's command lines. Grok's was checked with real headless calls on 2026-10-13 (its JSON fields and `--reasoning-effort`; it takes no `--json-schema`, so the schema goes in the prompt); Codex's and Cursor's have run no real call yet, and Muse's was checked with real calls on 2026-10-19 (see [Muse](#muse)). Grok's model, `grok-4.7`, and Muse's, `muse-wonder-full-1.0`, are already in the lane table, so they need only step 3. For Codex and Cursor:
1. find its models in the CLI's own list, and make one real headless call per role with the command line of its adapter (`plugins/aether/lib/harness/<name>.js`); correct the adapter's field names if the CLI's JSON differs;
2. copy `plugins/aether/config/lanes.json`, add the models to the harness's entry, each with `group`, `replaces`, `roles` and `levels` like agy's, and set `AETHER_LANES_CONFIG` to the copy in the shell that starts Claude Code. Codex, Grok and Muse take the level as their reasoning effort; Cursor ignores it for now;
3. add the harness to the `harnesses` option, such as `agy,codex`.

### The /harnesses panel

`/harnesses` opens a pane inside Claude Code that shows the delegation settings and the quota of each harness, so you do not have to run `aether-lane quota` by hand. It prints nothing to the transcript, calls no model and works even while the Lead is in the middle of a turn. `Esc` or `x` closes it, and `r` renews the readings. It is read-only: it changes no setting and has no toggle button, because the switch for lanes is the `lanes` option, in `/plugin configure aether@aether-plugin` or `/config`.

**Only in an Aether session.** The command exists only in a session started with `aether-lead` (agent `aether:lead`). In any other session it is not offered and nothing of it runs, so a plain session is as it was (see [what Aether adds to a normal session](../reference/components.md#what-aether-adds-to-a-normal-session)). The panel uses Claude Code's plugin UI API for mods, which is early access: its surface may change between Claude Code releases without notice, and if it stops working the panel simply does not draw. Nothing else of Aether depends on it. It was checked on Claude Code 2.1.302.

**What it shows**, from top to bottom:
- the title, `Aether · other harnesses`, and the options line, `lanes ON|OFF · router <mode> · decider <x> · seats <x> · harnesses <list>`. These are the values the session really uses, with the same validation and fallbacks as the libs: an unknown router mode is `record`, an unknown decider is `haiku`, unknown harness names are dropped and an empty list is `agy`. With lanes off, it says that nothing leaves Claude;
- the buttons `Renew readings` (`r`) and `Close` (`x`);
- for each harness (agy, grok, codex, cursor and muse), its version; `not enabled` when it is installed but not in `harnesses`, and `enabled, not installed` when it is listed but missing. For each quota group it gives the label and a status, `usable`, `dry until <date> UTC` (from the group's mark), `under the reserve` or `no reading`, and for each window (`weekly`, `5h`) a bar, the share left and `resets in <h>`, with how old the reading is and `stale` when it is;
- `Recent delegates`: the five newest delegations of the router log in the last 7 days, from any session, each with its age, the `harness/model/effort` it ran on, the task's description, and a mark when the router fell back. See [Delegate router](router.md#delegate-router).

Colours follow your theme: green for usable or enough, amber for low, dry, not enabled or stale, red for under the reserve, and dim for the rest.

**Layout.** From 110 columns in Claude Code's fullscreen layout the pane docks to the right of the transcript (about 67 columns wide at 150); below that, or on the main-screen layout, it sits above the prompt. It fits the room it has: when the full layout does not fit, it drops detail in steps and always keeps the title, the options line, the buttons and one line per enabled harness group. The arrow keys scroll the rest while the pane has the keyboard.

**Where the numbers come from, and how fresh they are.** The panel runs `aether-lane quota --json <harness>` once per harness, in parallel, so a slow agy does not hold back Grok (agy's reading launches `agy -p /usage` and takes 5 to 8 seconds; Grok's takes about a second), and `aether-report --delegates` with `--since` set to seven days ago. `r` reads the quota again with `--refresh` (Grok over ACP, agy's `/usage`); neither is a model call, and opening the pane costs no model tokens. After the first reads, a Grok reading older than 30 minutes is renewed once by itself.

**Safety.** Any text from a CLI or from a delegate's description is stripped of control characters and bidi overrides before it is drawn, because Claude Code refuses to draw a pane that holds a control character, and widths are measured in terminal cells. [Troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md#the-harnesses-panel-is-not-offered) covers a panel that is missing, cut or showing `Quota unavailable`. The sessions it was tried in are listed in [the handoff](../handoff/2026-10-17-harness-panel-0.12.3.md).

## Muse

Muse Code is Meta's coding agent, the `muse` CLI. It is the fifth lane harness, last in the `harnesses` list (`agy, codex, grok, cursor, muse`), and the swarm will send it units, by the same order and reserve as the others, once a model is in its table (see Parallel and models below). What follows is what the adapter does; the design is in [spec 008](../../specs/008-muse-harness/spec.md) and the Muse decision in [decisions](../decisions.md).

- **How a call runs.** In the unit's worktree, Aether runs `muse exec --json --provider meta --model <model>`, with `--reasoning-effort` when the lane table gives a level and `--output-schema` when the role asks for a structured answer. The call takes no standard input and asks nobody for anything: `--disable-approval`, `--disable-request-user-input` and `--disable-reminders` are on, and the run stops after 200 model steps. The reply, the failure reason and the token usage are read from the JSON records, and they come back as those of any other harness.
- **The sandbox stays on.** `--disable-approval` takes away only Muse's approvals. Muse's own sandbox runs the commands, and the workspace is not trusted, so Muse loads no project rules and starts no subagents on its own. The call carries none of `--yolo`, `--disable-sandbox`, `--trust-workspace` or `--api-key-stdin`. If the sandbox does not start, the call fails and the unit runs on the native agent; Aether never repeats it with the sandbox off.
- **Privacy.** Contributor mode is off on every call (`--contributor-mode off`) and no option turns it on, because Meta trains on the content of a run when it is on. A model whose id ends in `-contributor` is never put in the table nor called. `--no-foreign-personal-context` keeps Muse from loading rules, skills and memories that you keep for other tools.
- **Account.** Aether does not sign in for you: it never runs `muse login`, never opens Muse's credential files and does not use `META_API_KEY` (a lane's environment drops variables with `KEY`, `TOKEN` or `SECRET` in their name, and this harness is meant for your subscription, not a per-token key). Sign in to Muse yourself, once, in your own terminal.
- **Quota.** Aether does not read Muse's quota before a call. It knows it from what Muse reported at the end of its last call: the 5 hour window and the weekly one, kept in `lanes/quota/muse-observed.json` in the plugin's data directory. Before the first call the quota is unknown, which counts as usable and puts Muse after the groups that have a reading. A failure that carries Muse's own quota datum (never the text of the message) leaves the same 60-minute dry mark as the other harnesses (`dryMarkMinutes`), and a healthy observation from a later call lifts it.
- **Fallback.** Every failure sends the call to the native agent, at the level asked, without asking you: Muse not installed, `authRequired`, quota spent (only when the failure carries Muse's own quota datum), an `environmentError` from the sandbox, a timeout, `stepLimit`, a model your account does not give (`modelError`), output that cannot be read or lacks the object asked for, and any other non-zero exit. An `environmentError` leaves no mark and the quota is not counted as spent, so the next unit sent to Muse tries the sandbox again.
- **Parallel and models.** `maxParallel` is 2: two `muse exec` in different worktrees ran at once on 2026-10-19 and both came back clean. The table has one model, `muse-wonder-full-1.0`, the only one the CLI lists to the owner's account (`model/list` of `muse serve`), which Meta gives only on the Power and Maximum plans; an account without the plan is refused and the call runs native. It replaces Opus, as implementer and reviewer, **by the owner's decision**: Artificial Analysis names an effort for its only figure of this model, `max`, which the CLI does not take (it goes up to `xhigh`), so the rules of the models index would keep it out, and the entry carries the owner's words in `ownerDecision` (the lane-table check lets a model with no figure in only with that field). At `max` it scores 55.8 in intelligence and 0.57 in terminal-bench, against Opus 5.5 at `high` with 53.6 and 0.57; Aether's `xhigh` runs at Muse's `high`. `muse-spark-1.3` also answered a real call, but the CLI does not list it to this account, so it is not in the table. Add `muse` to `harnesses` and the swarm sends it units; `scripts/lane-smoke.js muse` calls it with the table's model, or the one you name, and spends quota, so run it on purpose.
- **Seen in real calls (2026-10-19, muse 1.4.11).** A clean call takes 5 to 12 seconds and about 54,000 input tokens for a one-line task, most of it Muse's own instructions. `allowance` arrives in the usage record in snake_case, with a `window` and a `weekly` block (`used_percent`, `resets_at_ms`) and no tier, and the quota the adapter keeps from it reads as the 5 hour window and the weekly one. `--output-schema` takes the roles' schemas, the sandbox runs inside WSL2, the subscription covers `muse exec`, and tool calls appear in the JSON records. Not seen: a failure in the middle of a run. Muse's own `--max-model-steps 1` did not give one: the run never ended and the lane's time limit killed it (the adapter passes 200).
