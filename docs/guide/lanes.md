# Lanes

Lanes run Aether's own calls on another coding plan you pay for, while that plan's quota lasts. The `lanes` and `harnesses` options that turn them on are in [Options](options.md#options); this page is how they behave.

## Running Aether's calls on other plans

### Lanes

A lane runs Aether's own calls on another coding plan the owner pays for, while that plan's quota lasts. agy, the Antigravity CLI, was the first; Codex, Grok and Cursor have adapters too. See the [lanes spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-08-lanes-design.md) and phase 2b of the [RADAR roadmap](../roadmap/2026-10-10-radar.md).

- **Which harnesses.** The plugin's `harnesses` option lists the harnesses lanes may use (`harnesses`, `agy` by default). Every one whose CLI is installed and whose table entry has a model for the call's native model and role is a candidate for that call. `aether-plan compile` records the candidates of each role and native model.
- **The order.** The quota decides among the candidates of one call, never the level: they serve the same role and replace the same native model, and a test checks the table against the models index (`plugins/aether/config/models/models.json`: current, offered by the harness, with a profile). The candidates go in this order: first the groups with a reading, the highest priority first; in a tie, the nearer reset, then the order of `harnesses`; then the groups with no reading, in the order of `harnesses`. A group under the reserve, or with a dry mark, is left out. The priority of a group is the share left of its longest window (the weekly one) divided by the hours to its reset, at least one hour: the quota most at risk of being lost goes first, and the shares of different plans compare equal. The workflow walks the order for each call: the first candidate whose pool has room takes it; if its start is unavailable, for any reason, the next is tried; the call runs native, at its own level, only when none is left.
- **Who takes a call.** `plugins/aether/config/lanes.json` names the external models of each harness. For agy: Gemini 3.8 Flash for Haiku implementers, and agy's own Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 for the rest; Gemini 4 Argon takes design seats only. agy's `high` runs Claude's `high` and `xhigh`; calls at `max`, and `ops`, are always native. For Grok: `grok-4.7` for Haiku implementers (checked against grok 1.0.41 on 2026-10-13; `grok-4.7-build-fast` is the same model faster and at twice the price, so it is not a lane). Codex and Cursor have entries without models: their model ids depend on your account and the CLI's version, so you add them once you have checked them (below).
- **Quota.** A group is used while every window of it, such as agy's 5 hours and weekly, stays above a 5% reserve (`reserve` in the table) and no dry mark holds it. A call that ends for quota, or a start that finds the group dry, leaves a dry mark in the plugin's data directory, so it outlives the wave and the run: until the group's reset when a reading with a balance shows the group under the reserve, and otherwise for 60 minutes (`dryMarkMinutes`), renewed if the next call in the group ends for quota again. A reading that launches a process, agy's, is kept for its life (`quotaLifeMinutes`: 10 minutes) and served to the starts and to `status`, so they launch none inside it; a finished agy call and `aether-lane quota` take a fresh reading, which renews the kept one. Grok's reading is not kept: its 30 minutes (`quotaLifeMinutes`) are the age after which its log line is stale and a wave refreshes it. Grok's weekly share stays valid until its week ends. Codex and Cursor report no quota, nor does Grok without a reading: each is tried after the groups that have one, until a call fails for quota.
- **agy's three quotas.** agy has an individual quota for Gemini 4 Argon, one for Gemini 3.8 Flash, and one for the Claude and GPT models, and its `/usage` lists only two groups, `Gemini Models` and `Claude and GPT models`. The table therefore has three groups for agy, `argon`, `flash` and `claude`; `groups` maps each to the `/usage` group it reads, so `argon` and `flash` both read `Gemini Models` and show the same windows, but each has its own dry mark, its own place in the order and its own choice. What tells which of them is gone is agy's refusal, `Individual quota reached. ... Resets in 163h22m27s.`: a lane call or a design seat that agy refuses leaves a dry mark on the group of the model it called, until the reset the message states (8 days at most), whatever `/usage` shows. That mark says in `aether-lane quota` that it comes from a refusal, and a reading with a balance does not lift it. A design seat skips a group a dry mark holds and goes to the next model of its list, or to the native agent. A mark file of a group the table no longer has, such as the old `gemini`, is ignored.
- **Fallback.** When a harness is missing, out of quota or fails to start, the same call goes to the next candidate, and to Aether's own agent when none is left, without spending the ladder. A lane that fails after it started goes to the native agent. `aether-plan compile` names each enabled harness that takes no call, and why.
- **Containment.** A lane works without permission prompts in the unit's worktree: agy with `--dangerously-skip-permissions`, Codex in its `workspace-write` sandbox, Grok with `--always-approve`, Cursor with `--force`. Git checks catch changes outside the task's Owns, to the integration worktree and branch, and from read-only roles. The lane's environment drops variables named like tokens, keys and secrets, points `gh`, `npm`, `docker`, `gcloud`, `aws` and `kubectl` away from your credentials, and sets git's `protocol.allow` to `never`, so a plain git command in the lane cannot reach a remote. Your home directory stays, because each CLI keeps its own login there. So this prevents accidents, not an agent that overrides these settings or goes looking for your files. Nothing undoes a network action, so you turn on each harness yourself.
- **Report.** `aether-report` shows, per group, its calls, the quota consumed, the share left of each window at the start and at the end of the run and the next reset; the dollars at list price, apart from native Claude; each harness's calls; and what every lane call chose (`lanes.run.choices`: the candidates in order with their priority, the lane that took the call or none, and why). The same choices are in the lane log, one entry per call. `aether-lane status <run>` gives, per group, what `aether-lane quota` does plus its dry mark, and, per role and native model, the order of the candidates.
- **Off for one run:** `aether-plan compile <plan.md> --no-lanes`.
- **Off by default.** Lanes run only when the plugin's `lanes` option is on (`lanes`, off by default; see [Options](options.md#options)).
- **Linux only.** Lanes need Linux, because stopping a lane's processes reads `/proc`.

**Reading the quota.** `aether-lane quota [<harness>...] [--refresh] [--json]` needs no run. For each harness, by default the installed ones, it prints each group's windows: the share left, the reset in UTC and local time, the hours left, the priority, whether the group is usable above the reserve and with no dry mark, and where the reading came from and how old it is. agy's reading is its `/usage`. **Grok's quota is read over ACP**: grok answers the request `_x.ai/billing` on `grok agent stdio` with the config its UI logs as `billing: fetched credits config` (about a second, no UI, no session and no model call), and the request also leaves that line in `~/.grok/logs/unified.jsonl`, where `status` and `start` read it; they call it stale after 30 minutes. `aether-lane quota --refresh`, and `aether-lane status <run> --refresh`, the quota step of each wave in the workflow, ask grok over ACP (10 seconds at most); if that gives no answer, an error or no config, the fallback opens grok's UI for a few seconds (20 at most) in an empty temporary directory, with no prompt, and removes the session folder grok made for that directory, and no other. A `status` run by hand does not refresh. A line without `creditUsagePercent` is read as 0% used, and says so wherever it is shown (spec 002, A3). Aether never refreshes inside a lane call.

**Adding a harness.** A harness is an adapter, `plugins/aether/lib/harness/<name>.js`, and an entry in the table, and its name goes in `HARNESS_NAMES` and `FACTORY` of `plugins/aether/lib/harness/index.js`, or the `harnesses` option drops it; the order rule does not change. The adapter's `quota()` gives, per group, its windows (`remaining` from 0 to 1 and `resetsAt`) or `unknown` when it cannot read them, which puts the harness after the groups with a reading. An adapter whose reading launches a process sets `quotaCost: 'process'`, and Aether keeps the reading for `quotaLifeMinutes`.

**Turning on Codex, Grok or Cursor.** Their adapters follow Spec Kit's command lines. Grok's was checked with real headless calls on 2026-10-13 (its JSON fields and `--reasoning-effort`; it takes no `--json-schema`, so the schema goes in the prompt); Codex's and Cursor's have run no real call yet. Grok's model, `grok-4.7`, is already in the lane table, so Grok needs only step 3. For Codex and Cursor:
1. find its models in the CLI's own list, and make one real headless call per role with the command line of its adapter (`plugins/aether/lib/harness/<name>.js`); correct the adapter's field names if the CLI's JSON differs;
2. copy `plugins/aether/config/lanes.json`, add the models to the harness's entry, each with `group`, `replaces`, `roles` and `levels` like agy's, and set `AETHER_LANES_CONFIG` to the copy in the shell that starts Claude Code. Codex and Grok take the level as their reasoning effort; Cursor ignores it for now;
3. add the harness to the `harnesses` option, such as `agy,codex`.

### The /harnesses panel

`/harnesses` opens a pane inside Claude Code that shows the delegation settings and the quota of each harness, so you do not have to run `aether-lane quota` by hand. It prints nothing to the transcript, calls no model and works even while the Lead is in the middle of a turn. `Esc` or `x` closes it, and `r` renews the readings. It is read-only: it changes no setting and has no toggle button, because the switch for lanes is the `lanes` option, in `/plugin configure aether@aether-plugin` or `/config`.

**Only in an Aether session.** The command exists only in a session started with `aether-lead` (agent `aether:lead`). In any other session it is not offered and nothing of it runs, so a plain session is as it was (see [what Aether adds to a normal session](../reference/components.md#what-aether-adds-to-a-normal-session)). The panel uses Claude Code's plugin UI API for mods, which is early access: its surface may change between Claude Code releases without notice, and if it stops working the panel simply does not draw. Nothing else of Aether depends on it. It was checked on Claude Code 2.1.302.

**What it shows**, from top to bottom:
- the title, `Aether · other harnesses`, and the options line, `lanes ON|OFF · router <mode> · decider <x> · seats <x> · harnesses <list>`. These are the values the session really uses, with the same validation and fallbacks as the libs: an unknown router mode is `record`, an unknown decider is `haiku`, unknown harness names are dropped and an empty list is `agy`. With lanes off, it says that nothing leaves Claude;
- the buttons `Renew readings` (`r`) and `Close` (`x`);
- for each harness (agy, grok, codex and cursor), its version; `not enabled` when it is installed but not in `harnesses`, and `enabled, not installed` when it is listed but missing. For each quota group it gives the label and a status, `usable`, `dry until <date> UTC` (from the group's mark), `under the reserve` or `no reading`, and for each window (`weekly`, `5h`) a bar, the share left and `resets in <h>`, with how old the reading is and `stale` when it is;
- `Recent delegates`: the five newest delegations of the router log in the last 7 days, from any session, each with its age, the `harness/model/effort` it ran on, the task's description, and a mark when the router fell back. See [Delegate router](router.md#delegate-router).

Colours follow your theme: green for usable or enough, amber for low, dry, not enabled or stale, red for under the reserve, and dim for the rest.

**Layout.** From 110 columns in Claude Code's fullscreen layout the pane docks to the right of the transcript (about 67 columns wide at 150); below that, or on the main-screen layout, it sits above the prompt. It fits the room it has: when the full layout does not fit, it drops detail in steps and always keeps the title, the options line, the buttons and one line per enabled harness group. The arrow keys scroll the rest while the pane has the keyboard.

**Where the numbers come from, and how fresh they are.** The panel runs `aether-lane quota --json <harness>` once per harness, in parallel, so a slow agy does not hold back Grok (agy's reading launches `agy -p /usage` and takes 5 to 8 seconds; Grok's takes about a second), and `aether-report --delegates` with `--since` set to seven days ago. `r` reads the quota again with `--refresh` (Grok over ACP, agy's `/usage`); neither is a model call, and opening the pane costs no model tokens. After the first reads, a Grok reading older than 30 minutes is renewed once by itself.

**Safety.** Any text from a CLI or from a delegate's description is stripped of control characters and bidi overrides before it is drawn, because Claude Code refuses to draw a pane that holds a control character, and widths are measured in terminal cells. [Troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md#the-harnesses-panel-is-not-offered) covers a panel that is missing, cut or showing `Quota unavailable`. The sessions it was tried in are listed in [the handoff](../handoff/2026-10-17-harness-panel-0.12.3.md).
