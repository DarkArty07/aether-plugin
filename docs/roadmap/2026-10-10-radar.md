## 5. Phase 1: the Lead, the Aether method on Spec Kit, superpowers out (items 1, 3 and 4)

### 5.1 What the Lead becomes

The owner's right hand and the accountable orchestrator:
- **It understands the intent first.** It converses with the owner, explores, and writes back its understanding, keeping the owner's words apart from its own assumptions. Once the owner confirms it, the Lead works on its own. Its questions before that confirmation are how it understands the intent; they are not the interruptions that C2 counts.
- **It answers for the whole result:** a good design, a correct implementation and a correct deployment. It verifies a deployment as it verifies the tests today. A deployment is still irreversible: it runs only with a grant or the owner's yes.
- **It decides who does each piece of work:** itself, one subagent, the swarm, or an external harness. It decides by quality, cost and time.
- **It advises without being asked.** Before a large piece of work, it tells the owner the better options, the scope and the estimated cost and time, taken from the history of finalized runs in `runs.jsonl`.
- **It writes; Opus judges.** The Lead writes the spec, and the Writer, on Sonnet, writes the plan and the tasks. The Judge, on Opus, reviews each document and rules: approve, revise or reject.
  - When the Judge asks for changes, the document is revised, at most twice. oh-my-claudecode's own critic expects plans to be rejected 7 times on average (`agents/critic.md:26`), so the loop needs a hard stop.
  - When a third review still does not approve, or when the Lead thinks the Judge is wrong, both positions and their cost go to the owner's list, and the work that does not depend on them goes on.
  - The Lead never silently overrides the Judge.
- **It assumes instead of asking.** What the owner's intent does not cover, the Lead assumes and records: the assumption, its basis, and whether it can be reversed. Only what is hard to reverse, surprising or a real trade-off goes to the owner's list, and meanwhile only its reversible side is built. It never asks what the code can answer: a cheap explorer reads first.
- **Its briefs are its work.** Every brief opens with:
  - the list of irreversible actions, each with what it is, why, what happens if the owner says no, how to revert it, and the exact command;
  - the assumptions, ranked by how likely the owner is to veto them.

  It also adds "Qué no verifiqué" and the five RADAR axes. The list is in every brief, not only in autonomous runs.

The route by size and risk replaces superpowers' brainstorming:

| Route | When | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | A small or reversible change | The Lead, or one subagent with a self-contained brief |
| Short design | A bounded change | The Lead writes a short design; the Judge reviews it when it touches security, data, concurrency or core architecture; then a subagent or the swarm builds it |
| Spec and plan | Architectural work | The Lead writes the spec, the Judge reviews it; the Writer writes the plan and the tasks, the Judge reviews them; the swarm builds them |

### 5.2 The Aether method on Spec Kit

- **Documents.** Each feature gets `specs/NNN-<topic>/spec.md`, `plan.md` and `tasks.md`. A project may keep one constitution at `.specify/memory/constitution.md`. This is Spec Kit's own layout, so other agents and Spec Kit users read it as it is.
- **An Aether preset, shipped inside the plugin** (`plugins/aether/speckit/`). It is Spec Kit's preset format: a `preset.yml` and commands in Markdown. It starts from Spec Kit's `lean` preset (MIT), and changes it:
  - **specify** never stops to ask. It derives the folder name, opens the spec with the C1–C5 criteria and their evidence, and records the assumptions and the irreversible actions;
  - **plan** adds an optional deployment section: the deploy command, and the check that proves it worked;
  - **tasks** keeps Spec Kit's checklist (`- [ ] T012 [P] [US1] … path`) as the index. Each swarm unit gets a section with the Aether block (Tier, Review, Area, Owns, Depends on) and the contract: the interfaces, the failing tests in full, and the acceptance. A unit may cover several checklist lines: each merge runs the whole test command, so a unit must be large enough to be worth one. **Tests are required.** Spec Kit makes them optional by default;
  - no **implement**: the swarm builds;
  - no **analyze** either: its report runs to 300–500 lines. The Judge replaces it.
- **How the Lead and the Writer use it.** The plugin ships the preset's commands as its own skills (`aether:specify`, `aether:plan` and `aether:tasks`), built from the same files that `preset.yml` names. No CLI is needed.
- **Spec Kit's CLI is optional.** Anyone who has it can install the Aether preset with `specify preset add`, and get the same commands in every harness Spec Kit supports.
- **This returns to the first decision.** Spec Kit was Aether's method until 2026-10-07 (D2), and `specs/` was the place for what to build (D14). superpowers replaced it then, for its test-driven development, verification and reviews. Those now live in Aether's own agents (implementer, supervisor, reviewer and Judge) and in phase 2a. What changed is what Aether needs: autonomy in place of human gates, and a format that other harnesses read. Spec Kit's presets let Aether adapt it; superpowers' gates cannot be switched off.

| 2b.2 | **Three adapters,** in this order: Codex, Grok, Cursor. Each runs headless and asks for JSON. Codex runs as `codex exec <prompt> --model <m> --json`. Grok runs as `grok -p <prompt> --always-approve --model <m> --output-format json`. Cursor runs as `cursor-agent -p --trust --approve-mcps --force <prompt> --model <m> --output-format json`. **Each must return a result that validates against its role's schema,** as agy does with `--json-schema` today (`lib/harness/agy.js`). **Safety.** These CLIs work without asking permission:
- Spec Kit runs Cursor with `--force`, Grok with `--always-approve` and agy with `--dangerously-skip-permissions`, and leaves Codex to its own sandbox;
- oh-my-claudecode also bypasses Codex's sandbox;
- Aether already runs agy with `--dangerously-skip-permissions`.

Aether's protection covers files only. An external CLI runs in the unit's transactional worktree, a change outside the paths the task owns undoes the call (`lib/lane-work.js` checks git afterwards), and it is never given the integration worktree. Nothing undoes a network action, such as a push or a publication made with the owner's credentials. `aether-guard` does not see these CLIs either: it hooks Claude Code's own tools.

So phase 2b adds three rules:
- each CLI runs in its own sandbox, with the network off, wherever it has one;
- the owner's credentials (tokens and keys) are removed from the lane's environment;
- `git push` is disabled in the worktrees a lane works in.

The remaining risk is the owner's to accept. Each harness ships off, and the owner turns it on, one by one: item 5 of the owner's list.

**Done when:** a fixture run sends implementer calls to Codex, Grok and Cursor while their quota lasts. The report shows each harness's quota and cost, no lane is dry without notice, and the units pass review at the same rate as native ones.
