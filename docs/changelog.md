# Changelog

## Status

In `main`, not published yet (next release after 0.17.1): the owner's design decisions of 2026-10-21, built ([decisions](decisions.md) A77 to A83). A merge the mod cuts at 600 s is not merged until its tests pass, and the next merge of that unit reruns only the tests (A77); the final review runs once per run, when a launch ends with every unit merged, and otherwise says `deferred` (A78); a retry after a missing report splits the brief by owned file (A79); the design seats use the author's family as a reserve, choose the scarce seat first, and keep Grok out of the Judge's seat (A80); a correction round edits only what the Judge flagged (A81); a delegate or a Writer that takes over continues from the failed one's work (A82). The router's criterion is postponed (A83).

Version 0.17.1: the price of `grok-4.7` ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-22-session-cost-0.17.0.md), [issue 77](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/77)). The first measure of 0.17.0 found that a session that used Grok left those calls out of its dollars, because `config/prices.json` had no row for the model. What changed since 0.17.0:
- **The row.** `grok-4.7` is priced at OpenRouter's list for its xAI endpoint (checked 2026-10-22): US$ 2 per million tokens in, 6 out, 0.50 for a cached read. OpenRouter lists no price for a cache write, so a write is priced as input, the way the Muse rows are. The `--run` and `--spend` reports now count a Grok call in dollars, and so does the cost that an autonomous run records against its ceiling, which can now be reached sooner when the Lead's work goes to Grok. Measured on the session that built 0.17.0: its 11 Grok calls (the Writer rounds of two designs and the swarm's calls) come to US$ 18.69 at list price, and the session's total goes from US$ 29.09 to US$ 48.53.
- **Known and not changed.** Grok's delegations through the router record zero tokens in the journal (`usage` is all zeros, as in session `252123d1`), so they still add no dollars, and `unpriced` no longer warns about them. A model id is matched by its listed id and a suffix, so `grok-4.7-build-fast`, which costs twice as much, would be priced at the base rate if a lane ever used it: give it a row of its own then.

Version 0.17.0: the spend of a whole session, and the fixes of the open bugs ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-22-session-cost-0.17.0.md), [autonomy](guide/autonomy.md), [lanes](guide/lanes.md), [decisions](decisions.md) A74 to A76, [spec 012](../specs/012-session-cost/spec.md)). The owner asked how much a whole session spends, with several swarms and resumed after it was closed, and in dollars, because a quota percentage cannot be attributed to one of several sessions working at once. What changed since 0.16.0:
- **`aether-report --spend [--session <id>]`.** One figure for a session at list price: the total, the Lead's and the agents', by model, by model and effort and by role, with the first and last request, an approximate count of resumptions and the calls to other harnesses (the lane log and the router's delegations), counted once and listed apart. A model with no price is listed as unpriced and never counted as zero. Without `--session` it reports the session that wrote last.
- **The 30-day prune keeps it.** `pruneUsage` and `pruneLog` write a summary (`spend/<id>.json`) before they delete a session's files, so a session resumed after the prune adds to what it had spent, and a second prune adds to the summary and never replaces it.
- **`/spend`.** A mod command like `/harnesses` that shows the figure of the session in a pane, with no model call and no line in the transcript. It does not exist outside an Aether session.
- **The closing report carries it.** Before the Lead deletes worktrees and temporary files, and before it pushes, its report gives the session's spend with the Lead's and the agents' share and the models that have no price.
- **Fixes since 0.16.0**, merged to `main` before this release: a harness has no limit on its parallel calls and the cupo is gone (A74, issue 71); a reset time that a provider states is a hint, and a dry pair is tried again every hour (A75, issue 63); a stale harness that cannot serve a call is neither renewed nor waited for (issue 35); a native supervisor or reviewer and a design seat round have time caps (issues 16 and 62); the suite scrubs the `AETHER_*` variables it reads (issue 69); GitHub issues are the only list.
- **Known and not changed.** `grok-4.7` has no price row, so its calls count as calls and add no dollars; the minor findings of the build are [issue 77](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/77), and an Examiner that ends without `exam.json` is [issue 76](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/76).

Version 0.16.0: a quota brake for autonomous runs, and a workflow that is interrupted keeps its work ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-21-quota-brake-0.16.0.md), [autonomy](guide/autonomy.md), [decisions](decisions.md) A73, [spec 011](../specs/011-quota-brake/spec.md)). On 2026-10-21 Claude's own session limit ran out in the middle of a swarm and the autonomous run waited 6.4 hours for the owner. What changed since 0.15.0:
- **The brake.** When the five-hour window of the account reaches the threshold (the plugin option `quotabrake`, 95 by default) the owner gets one warning, and an autonomous run pauses: `aether-autonomy status` reports `stop: "quota"`, the run does not end, the Lead stops what runs after saving it, and the paused time is not worked time. The weekly window only warns, and so does a session with no run.
- **It resumes by itself.** A timer armed for the reset hour sends the Lead the plugin's own message; a session that starts during the pause finds the paused run and re-arms it.
- **Only his words turn it off.** `aether-autonomy brake-off --quote` takes the owner's own words, checked against his messages, for that run.
- **An interrupted workflow keeps its work.** `aether-wt save` commits each unit's unsaved work on its branch, a sweep of an interrupted run keeps the branches, and `aether-plan compile --run` leaves the merged units out of a relaunch. Pushing saved branches is the option `brakepush`, off by default.
- **Known and not changed.** A delegate or a design seat round that loses its pair mid-task is still redone from scratch, not continued ([issue 049](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/68)); the time issues 043 to 046 and 048 are open.

Version 0.15.0: a delegate task that edits files goes first to another harness ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-21-delegates-that-edit-0.15.0.md), [lanes](guide/lanes.md), [decisions](decisions.md) A72, [spec 010](../specs/010-delegates-that-edit/spec.md)). Until now every delegate that edited files ran on Claude, because an external delegate worked on a copy that was thrown away, which is against the owner's rule that the work goes to other harnesses first and Claude's quota is the reserve. It builds on 0.14.5 (A70 and A71). What changed since 0.14.5:
- **`aether-lane delegate`.** The command makes a unit worktree, picks the pair that reaches the points of the Claude pair named in `--model` and `--effort`, runs the implementer there, runs the charge's tests, has a second pair review the diff read-only, and answers `done` with the branch to merge, `no-candidate` or `off`. A pair that refuses or fails is replaced by the next one, its patch is kept, and the `cupo` of each harness is counted across processes.
- **The Lead's rule.** With superdelegation on, the bootstrap sends every editing delegate task, of any size, through the command in the background; the Lead merges with `aether-wt merge`, and a native Agent call takes the task only after `no-candidate`, with the line `AETHER-TASK: <id>` in its brief.
- **The measure.** The router journal keeps `aetherTask` and `taskClass`, and `aether-report --delegates` lists the editing delegations that ran on Claude with no `no-candidate` behind them.
- **The option text, `router.md`, `lanes.md` and the components page** say that a task that edits goes out by the command.
- **Known and not changed.** The router does not refuse an editing Agent call; the Lead's rule and the measure carry the guarantee. The final review of a whole package still runs on Claude when no pair reaches its points.

Version 0.14.5: Muse takes the schemas of the seats and the swarm, and a failed design seat asks for the next pair ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-21-external-seats-0.14.5.md), [design seats](guide/design-seats.md), [decisions](decisions.md) A70, issues [039](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/58), [040](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/59) and [041](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/60)). Running the design of spec 010 showed that every round Muse took failed and went to Claude, and that a design seat that failed went to Claude without trying another pair. What changed since 0.14.4:
- **Muse gets a strict copy of every schema.** The adapter writes a copy of the call's schema in which every object says `additionalProperties: false` and lists all its keys as required, which Muse's provider demands. All eight role schemas answered clean on real Muse calls.
- **A design seat that fails asks for the next pair.** `aether-lane seat-start` and `seat` take `--exclude`; `/aether:design` starts the round again with the pairs already tried excluded, up to three external tries, and the native agent does the round only when no pair is left. The record of a round lists each try.
- **A wait that reports the round's own exit is read.** The ops agent of a real run returned the exit of a round that did not answer (3 or 4) as the exit of `aether-lane seat-wait`, and `/aether:design` threw the wait away twelve times and gave the round to the native agent, so the next pair was never asked ([issue 042](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/61)). The wait is now read from the JSON it prints, whatever exit is reported.
- **A swarm call that fails asks for the next pair.** `/aether:run` offers a lane call that began and failed (or ended for quota) to the next ranked pair, up to three pairs in all, before the native agent takes it; an implementer's next pair is told what the earlier ones left in the worktree ([decisions](decisions.md) A71, [issue 041](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/60)).
- **The final review's slices ask the lanes.** When no pair takes the whole package, each slice (`final-review-slice-<n>`) is offered to the pairs before the native reviewer, and the result lists who took each slice (`slicesBy`).

Version 0.14.4: an Argon refusal no longer keeps Flash out ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-21-split-argon-flash-0.14.4.md), [lanes](guide/lanes.md), [decisions](decisions.md) A69, [issue 038](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/57)). agy's lane table had one quota key for Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 3.8 Flash, so a refusal of Argon marked Flash dry for 60 minutes too. What changed since 0.14.3:

- **A refusal marks one key.** Argon refused leaves Flash usable for the swarm, the design seats and the router, and the other way round.
- **The old key is ignored.** A mark of `gemini` is no mark for anyone, and a reading saved with the old keys is replaced in the background, so agy has no reading for a few seconds after the update.

Version 0.14.3: a pair that ran out of time is kept out of the router's choice, and the delegates' work is bounded ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-21-delegate-time-limits-0.14.3.md), [router](guide/router.md#fallback), [decisions](decisions.md) A68, issues [035](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/54), [036](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/55) and [037](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/56)). Watching 0.14.2 showed that every audit sent to Grok 4.7 at `high` was killed at 420 seconds and rerun on Claude. What changed since 0.14.2:
- **The brief bounds the work.** Every external delegate is told to use about 20 tool calls and then answer with the best it has, saying what it did not check.
- **A pair that ran out of time is out for 60 minutes.** The mark is per pair (`router/slow/`), not per quota group, so Grok at `low` keeps serving; the router excludes the pair from its choice and the chain skips it. A call killed at its limit is `failed`, never `quota` or a refusal at the start, and its outcome says `timedOut` and `slowUntil`.
- **An internal error of the pair step is journaled as one.** The decision's fallback reads `internal: …` and has no choice, as the guide said; before, the error's message stood where the choice goes.
- **Small faults.** An exception inside an attempt of the chain leaves a failed attempt with its outcome; agy's stated reset is read in either case of the units and with spaces between the parts.

Version 0.14.2: an external delegate that is refused at its start tries the next ranked candidate ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-21-delegate-next-candidate-0.14.2.md), [router](guide/router.md#fallback), [decisions](decisions.md) A67, [issue 034](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/45)). The owner saw two delegates fall to Claude when agy refused Gemini 4 Argon with Grok and Muse still ranked next, and called it a design error. What changed since 0.14.1:
- **Next candidate before Claude.** When an external delegate is refused at its start, because its quota is gone and it used no tokens or because its harness is missing, `aether-route delegate` tries the next candidate the decision ranked (`lib/delegate-chain.js`): at most three pairs, skipping any whose quota group holds a live mark, and only while the delegate's 570 seconds leave a CLI of at least 120 seconds. A call that used tokens, ran out of time, changed a file or met an uncommitted checkout still goes to Claude, as before.
- **The decision saves its candidates.** The external file carries `alternates`, the `next` entries of the ranking with their model, effort and quota group.
- **A refusal is `quota`.** agy's «Individual quota reached» ends as `quota`, not `failed`, so the report tells a refusal from a crash.
- **The record shows every try.** The journal has one outcome per try, numbered `attempt`; `aether-report --delegates` lists them in `attempts`, adds their time up, and keeps `ranOn` and `fallback` from the last. The answer the Lead reads names the pair that gave it and the pairs that refused first.

Version 0.14.1: superdelegation reaches the Lead's delegate tasks ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-21-router-superdelegation-0.14.1.md), [router](guide/router.md#quota), [options](guide/options.md), [decisions](decisions.md) A66). The owner asked that the traffic of the delegated agents also go to other harnesses. What changed since 0.14.0:
- **The router asks the one rule.** For a delegate task that only reads, the router decides how strong the task must be, which is the Claude rung it would run on, and asks the function of superdelegation (`lib/superdelegation.js`) which external pair reaches the points of that rung: the points of the pair with one less at most, the quota whose weekly window renews first, then the cheapest per task. The router's own choice of a harness, its table of rungs for agy and Grok and its tie by balance are gone, and so is the second quota rule. The decision of the journal keeps the candidates of the function and the reason each one did not take the call.
- **Muse takes delegates.** The router could only choose agy and Grok; it now chooses any harness of `harnesses` that has a pair, Muse included, and `aether-route delegate` runs a read-only delegate on Muse.
- **The router commands by default with superdelegation on.** The `router` option has a new value, `auto`, which is its default: `command` with superdelegation on and `record` with it off, and a mode you set holds. You no longer choose between `record`, `advise` and `command` to make delegates leave Claude.
- **The text of `superdelegation` says what it covers.** The swarm, the design seats and the Lead's delegate tasks that only read. A delegate task that edits files stays on Claude, because an external delegate works on a copy that is thrown away: sending those out is a spec of its own.
- **The fallback is the call's own rung.** An external delegate that does not end ok runs on Claude on the rung the call would have run on, which is the reference of the pair that failed; before, it was the cheapest rung of the level.

Version 0.14.0: superdelegation, one switch for everything that is not the Lead ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-20-superdelegation-0.14.0.md), [lanes](guide/lanes.md#lanes), [options](guide/options.md), [decisions](decisions.md) A65). It closes [issue 031](https://github.com/DarkArty07/aether-plugin/issues/52). What changed since 0.13.0:
- **One option, `superdelegation`, off by default.** It replaces `lanes` and the `native`, `balanced` and `robust` profiles of `seats`. Off, every call that is not the Lead's runs on Claude Code, as with `lanes` off and `seats` native. On, each swarm call (implementer, supervisor, final review) and each design round (the Writer's drafts and corrections, the Judge, the Examiner) goes to another harness first, and Claude is only the fallback, without asking the owner. A value saved before is not broken in silence: `lanes` on counts as `superdelegation` on, `seats` is ignored, and the session start and the report say so. `lanes` and `seats` stay in the manifest only as legacy keys.
- **A call asks for points, not for a name.** Each call asks for the Artificial Analysis intelligence of the Claude pair it would run on, a model at an effort (Sonnet `high` is 46.8 and Sonnet `xhigh` is 51.9). An external pair that reaches it within one point may take it, and it runs at its own effort, never translated (agy used to run `xhigh` as `high`). A pair with no measured intelligence or cost per task does not enter, unless the owner decided it does: Muse Wonder Full enters as Opus `high`, by his decision.
- **The order: the quota that renews first, then the cheapest per task.** Among the pairs that reach the points, the one whose quota's weekly window renews first goes first, then the lower cost per task; within a quota, a pair that gives no more points than another and costs more is never used. Speed decides nothing. A retry asks for its own pair and never reruns on the one that failed. A full harness is waited for, up to 10 minutes, before the call runs native. Nobody reviews its own work: the supervisor, the final review, the Judge and the Examiner are never the model that wrote what they check.
- **One table, one function.** `config/pairs.json` holds 29 pairs of model and effort with the Artificial Analysis figures read on 2026-10-19 (intelligence and speed from its API; cost per task and tokens from its model pages, because the API does not give them). `lib/superdelegation.js` chooses. The swarm asks it through the new `aether-lane pick`, `took` and `pause`, and the design seats through `lib/seats.js`. Gone: the hand-written `replaces` and `levels` of the lane table, `designSeats`, the swarm's own order of candidates, `aether-lane choice`, the fixed native rule for `max` and for the Writer's corrections. The final review goes through the same rule before it is sliced.
- **The report says why.** `aether-report` gives, per call, the pair it asked for, the pair that took it and why, how long it took, and the share the plans covered.
- **The cap of each harness is measured.** `scripts/lane-cupo.js <harness>` ramps simultaneous calls and writes the result in the lane table. With real calls on 2026-10-20: agy took all 8 (its cap goes from 4 to 8); grok and muse took 2, and the third call came back four to five times slower, so their cap stays 2.
- **The bug issues.** The branch `fix/bug-issues`, built by another agent, is in. A unit's merge is checked in git before it is called blocked (002); an ops reply that says it did not run the command is asked once more (004); the development tree's bins run by path (008); the agent budget counts model agents only (013); the owner's message sent in the middle of a turn counts as his words (014); a model or argument error is a failure whatever the quota reading says (015); a seat never tries a model its harness does not list (016); revoking a one-time grant removes only that one (017); the final review of more than three units runs in slices, and the Judge and the reviewers get a reading plan (011, 022); an implementer commits as it goes and reports before its budget ends (003, 010); a lane call's row names the harness and the model (032); and a delegate that waits on its own background work no longer counts as something that will wake the Lead (033). Closed: 003, 004, 011, 013, 014, 015, 016, 017. Partly fixed, with what is left in each issue: 002, 005, 008, 010, 022, 023, 025, 032. Not fixable in the plugin: 012; 033 waits on Claude Code.
- **What did not change.** The router, the Lead, `harnesses`, and Muse's adapter and its quota by observation.
- **To use it.** After `claude plugin update aether@aether-plugin`, `--config superdelegation=true` turns it on; it is off until then, because the external CLIs run without permission prompts.

Version 0.13.0: Muse Code, the fifth lane harness ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-19-muse-harness-0.13.0.md), [guide](guide/lanes.md#muse), [decisions](decisions.md) A64). What changed since 0.12.8:
- **A new harness, `muse`.** It is the fifth name of the `harnesses` option, last in every fixed list (`agy, codex, grok, cursor, muse`). Aether runs `muse exec --json` in the unit's own worktree and brings back a clean answer or the reason of the failure, plus the usage, as it does for the others; a unit that asks for structured output gets the object asked for.
- **The quota is known by observation.** Aether does not read Muse's quota before a call. It keeps the last `allowance` that `muse exec --json` reported, with a 5 hour and a weekly window and no synthetic one, in `lanes/quota/muse-observed.json`. Without a reading the quota is unknown, which counts as usable and goes behind the groups that have one. The order and the reserve are the other harnesses'. A refusal that carries Muse's own `quota` datum leaves the usual 60-minute mark, and a healthy reading taken after it lifts the mark.
- **Every failure falls to the native agent, without a question.** Not installed, no session, quota spent, a sandbox that does not start, a timeout, unreadable output, and a model the account does not give: each one runs the call on the native agent at the level asked. A test covers each shape.
- **Privacy.** Contributor mode is off on every call and `--no-foreign-personal-context` is on; a model ending in `-contributor` is never put in the table nor called. Aether never signs in for the owner, never opens Muse's credential files and never uses `META_API_KEY`.
- **The sandbox stays on.** The call carries none of `--yolo`, `--disable-sandbox`, `--trust-workspace` or `--api-key-stdin`; only the approvals are off. If the sandbox does not start, the call fails and the unit runs on the native agent; Aether does not retry with the sandbox off.
- **`maxParallel` is 2, and the table has one model.** Two `muse exec` at once came back clean in a real test, so `maxParallel` is 2. The model is `muse-wonder-full-1.0`, the only one the CLI lists to the owner's account, which Meta gives only on the Power and Maximum plans. It replaces Opus (implementer and reviewer) by the owner's decision, written in its entry as `ownerDecision`: Artificial Analysis scores it only at `max`, which the CLI does not take, so the rules of the models index alone would keep it out, and the lane-table check lets a model with no figure in only with that field. Add `muse` to `harnesses` and the swarm sends it units.
- **Profiles and prices.** `config/models/profiles.json` and `config/prices.json` hold the three models Meta documents, `muse-spark-1.3`, `muse-wonder-1.0` and `muse-wonder-full-1.0`, though the CLI lists only the last. The prices come from a web summary and are still to be checked against Meta's pages.
- **A test checks the lists.** A test over the tracked files fails on a line that names three of the four older harnesses without `muse`, or that counts the harnesses with a number, so a list cannot go stale again. The router, the design seats and the texts that say «other harness» in general are the named exceptions.
- **Two small changes in shared code.** `runCall` now parses the output before it reads the quota after the call, because Muse's quota is in the output; and a quota reading taken before a mark no longer lifts it. The second change applies to every harness that keeps its reading in a file: grok's log line, which carries its own time, no longer lifts a mark when it is older than the mark.
- **What did not change.** The behavior of agy, codex, grok and cursor; the delegate router; the design seats; the quota order; and the options, except the description of `harnesses`, which now names `muse`.
- **Real calls.** On 2026-10-19 the Lead made real calls through the adapter, with the owner's permission (muse 1.4.11, `muse-wonder-full-1.0` and `muse-spark-1.3`): clean structured answers in 5 to 12 seconds, the sandbox runs in WSL2, the subscription covers `muse exec`, `--output-schema` takes the roles' schemas, and `allowance` has the shape the adapter assumed (a real capture is now a test fixture). One fix came out of it: the reason of a failed exit is the first stderr line that is not one of Muse's own `muse: …` notices. Not seen: a failure in the middle of a run; `--max-model-steps 1` made Muse hang until the time limit, and the adapter passes 200.
- **Publishing is the owner's.** The work is on the branch `feat/muse-harness`; nothing is merged, tagged or published until the owner runs `claude plugin update aether@aether-plugin` after the release.

Version 0.12.7: the `/aether:onboard` skill ([handoff](handoff/2026-10-19-onboard-skill-0.12.7.md), [components](reference/components.md)). What changed since 0.12.5:
