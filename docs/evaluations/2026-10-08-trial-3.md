# Trial 3: the Lead and the Designer against an Opus main session (2026-10-08)

**Question.** When the Lead on Sonnet holds the conversation and the Designer on Opus writes the spec and the plan, does the quality of the design drop? This is the gate of the [Lead and Designer spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-08-lead-and-designer-design.md) (R3, A17), and Task 7 of its [plan](../superpowers/plans/2026-10-08-lead-and-designer.md).

**Verdict: no quality drop was found, and the owner kept the split.**
- **Quality:**
  - both plans pass `aether-plan check`;
  - the blind judge found that the Lead and Designer design captures all ten items of the owner's hidden intent, against nine for the Opus main session, which contradicted the tenth;
  - the blind pairwise judgment is a tie: in each order, the judge preferred the design it read first.
- **Cost and time:** in this short design session the split saved nothing:
  - 4% more at OpenRouter prices ($10.67 against $10.23);
  - 5 minutes more (44 min against 39).

  That condition of A17 is not met here. The owner ruled on quality: "si no hay una caída clara de calidad entonces lo hacemos así". The saving the split targets is in long sessions, where the main session re-reads hundreds of thousands of tokens at every step (spec §2); this trial does not measure it.

## Setup

**The request.** Design "effort per tier in the swarm", the owner's next sub-project, on a clone of this repository.
- The clone is reset to `1597ac7` (v0.1), with no remote and no unreachable objects.
- The owner's [opening message](trial-3/opening.md) is the same in both runs: their own words, with the Artificial Analysis table.

**The simulated owner.** A Sonnet session ([`owner.py`](lead-designer-smoke/owner.py)) with a [hidden brief](trial-3/brief.md), written before the runs:
- the intent;
- ten items the design must capture;
- the stop condition: the plan is committed, passes `aether-plan check`, and the session offers execution.

**The configurations:**

| | Baseline | New |
|---|---|---|
| Plugin | v0.1, at `1597ac7` | this branch |
| Main session | Opus 5.5 at `max`, which designs itself | the Lead: Sonnet 5.5 at `xhigh` |
| Designer | — | `aether:designer`: Opus 5.5 at `max`, in its own context |

Both ran headless through [`drive.py`](lead-designer-smoke/drive.py) and [`run.sh`](trial-3/harness/run.sh), one after the other, with:
- MCP connectors off, bypassed permissions and the advisor off;
- a 30-turn cap and a $40 cap.

**The quality measures, all blind:**
- **q1:** `aether-plan check` on the plan the session wrote.
- **q2:** an Opus judge marks each brief item as captured, missed or contradicted, in each design.
- **q3:** an Opus judge scores both designs, once in each order.
- **q4:** the owner's blind preference.

[`blind.py`](trial-3/harness/blind.py) gave the designs neutral names, X and Y, with seed 363998, and [`judge.py`](trial-3/harness/judge.py) ran q2 and q3.

## Results

| | Baseline (Y) | New (X) |
|---|---|---|
| Stopped on | `DONE`, after 8 owner messages | `DONE`, after 6 owner messages |
| Wall time | 39 min 05 s | 44 min 15 s |
| Cost, Claude Code's `total_cost_usd` | $10.23 | $12.07 |
| Cost at OpenRouter prices (`aether-report price`) | **$10.23**, all Opus | **$10.67**: Opus $9.88, Sonnet $0.79 |
| Opus output tokens | 253,539 | 262,221 (the Designer) |
| Main-session requests | 49 on Opus (6.1 per owner message) | 24 on Sonnet (4.0 per owner message) |
| Spec / plan written | 23 KB / 64 KB | 17 KB / 58 KB, 1,185 lines, 4 tasks in one wave |
| **q1:** `aether-plan check` | pass | pass |
| **q2:** brief items captured | 9 of 10, and item 7 contradicted | **10 of 10** |
| **q3:** judge reads X first | 4 / 3 / 3 / 4 | **4 / 5 / 4 / 4, preferred** |
| **q3:** judge reads Y first | **4 / 4 / 5 / 5, preferred** | 3 / 4 / 3 / 4 |

The q3 scores are correctness, completeness, design and executability.

The two cost figures differ because Claude Code bills Sonnet's cache reads at twice OpenRouter's rate, as trial 2 found. The Lead is cache-read heavy, so Claude Code's figure overstates the new run's cost.

**Reading the results:**
- **q2.**
  - Item 7 says that the workflow passes the effort on each `agent()` call, with the agent file's effort only the default.
  - The baseline passes it to implementers and supervisors, but not to ops or the final reviewer. Those keep their agent file's effort, which gives the same values. The judge counted it as a contradiction; it is a small one.
- **q3.** In each order the judge preferred the design it read first, and its scores flipped with the order. That is position bias, which is why it runs in both orders. Neither design won both, so q3 is a tie.
- **The judge's notes:**
  - **The new design:** an explicit, configurable ladder of model-and-effort rungs, from Haiku `xhigh` ×2 to Sonnet `high`, Sonnet `xhigh` and Opus `xhigh`; the effort passed on every call; the report showing effort; exact tests. Weak points: one large coupled task, a fixed path in its smoke run, and the supervisor fixed at `xhigh`.
  - **The baseline design:** an effort-first ladder derived from attempt counts, with no new state; a per-task Review line to raise the supervisor's effort; tighter waves; a committed transcript checker with tests. Weak point: item 7.
- **q4.** The owner did not pick X or Y. Given q1, q2 and q3, the owner ruled that there was no clear drop in quality, and kept the split.

## How the new configuration behaved

- **The route.** The Lead classified the opening as a bounded part (Haiku at `xhigh`) and an architectural one (dynamic effort).
- **The understanding.**
  - The simulated owner corrected the scope: everything now, in one design.
  - The Lead wrote its understanding back and waited for the confirmation before handing off, as A13 requires.
- **The rounds.** The Designer worked them as the protocol says: approaches, seven design sections, the spec, then the plan. The Lead relayed each round, and passed the owner's answers back with `SendMessage`.
- **The cost.** The Lead stayed cheap: $0.79 for the whole session. Almost all the cost is the Designer's work at `max`, which is the design work itself; the baseline paid it too.

## The interrupted run

The first run of the new configuration hit the owner's subscription session limit 11 minutes in, during the Designer's first round: "You've hit your session limit · resets 3:40pm".
- Up to then it had cost $1.90.
- It reran in full after the reset, and only the rerun counts.
- Its record is in [`results/interrupted`](trial-3/results/interrupted).

## Threats to validity

- **One run per configuration.** Model sampling varies, so a small difference between the designs could be chance. A tie means no drop was detected, not that the designs are equal.
- **The simulated owner answers what each session asks,** so the two conversations differed. For example, the new run's owner corrected the scope early.
- **The judge is Opus,** the same model that designed in both runs. Blindness and the two orders reduce its bias but do not remove it.
- **The evaluator wrote the brief and designed the split.** The brief was fixed before the runs.
- **A design document is a proxy.** The quality of a design shows when it is built. Trial 2 found an Aether build whose tests all passed, but whose game was weaker.
- **The owner's read was not fully blind:** the evaluator had mentioned the new plan's size during the run.
- **One short design session.** It cannot show the saving the split targets, which needs long sessions.

## Follow-ups

1. **Measure long sessions in real use.** The usage meter records cost by role (Lead, Designer, swarm). Compare the owner's next long session on v0.2 with the main session of the session that built v0.1: $111, at a median of 651K tokens per request.
2. **The Designer's effort.** At `max` the Designer wrote 262K output tokens for one spec and one plan, as much as the baseline's Opus.
   - Artificial Analysis rates Opus 5.5 at `xhigh` 1.6 points lower on its index, and about 7 times faster to the first answer token.
   - Changing the effort is one line in `agents/designer.md`. A rerun of this trial would measure it.
